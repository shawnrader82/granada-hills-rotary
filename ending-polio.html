About the Effort to End Polio

Since 1985, Rotary has been at the center of the global effort to eradicate polio, helping immunize billions of children and reducing cases by more than 99 percent. Granada Hills Rotary plays its part by raising funds, building awareness, and supporting Rotary International’s PolioPlus program.

Through events, club giving, and member outreach, we help ensure that vaccines, health workers, and community educators reach the few remaining places where polio is still a threat—and that the disease never returns where it has been eliminated.

Our Club’s Polio Focus

  • Host awareness events such as World Polio Day presentations.
  • Engage youth and community partners in “End Polio Now” activities.
  • Share Rotary stories about progress in remaining polio-endemic regions.

Why This Project Matters

We’re This Close to Ending a Disease

Polio once paralyzed hundreds of thousands of children every year across dozens of countries. Today, thanks to vaccination campaigns led by Rotary and our partners, wild poliovirus remains in only a few places and case counts are at historic lows.

Finishing the job is critical. As long as polio exists anywhere, children everywhere remain at risk. Continued funding, vaccination, and surveillance are what prevent the disease from returning to countries that are now polio-free.

Rotary’s Global Partnership

  • Works alongside the World Health Organization, UNICEF, and other partners.
  • Supports vaccination campaigns and community health workers.
  • Funds disease surveillance and laboratory testing in high-risk areas.
  • Advocates with governments to sustain long-term eradication efforts.

Our Club’s Contribution

Local Commitment, Global Impact

Granada Hills Rotary contributes each year to PolioPlus through club giving and special campaigns. Those dollars are combined with support from other Rotary clubs and partners around the world, multiplying the impact on vaccination and surveillance.

For our members, supporting End Polio Now is a way to be part of a once-in-history public health achievement—and to show future generations what service on a global scale can accomplish.
